Two small moments, made beautiful.

Mac Duo lives in your menu bar, uses almost no power, and stays out of the way until the moment it matters.

Follows your lid in real time

Mac Duo reads the hinge sensor built into your MacBook 60 times a second. The fold starts early and tracks your hand, if you pause it stays put, and lifting the lid clears it in an instant.

Ignores screen adjustments

Tilting the screen to cut glare or get a better angle never sets it off. It only folds for a real close, judged by speed, distance and angle.

Glossy power-off fade

Hold the power button and a specular sheen sweeps across your screen. It frosts, recedes like a pane of glass and fades to black. It also plays on Shut Down and Restart.

Closing vs. adjusting

Every reading from the hinge sensor goes through a small state machine. A fold starts only when all of these hold:

1
The lid is heading down past 85°.

That's early enough that the fold plays across almost the whole close.

2
It's moving shut quickly.

At least 60°/s, measured with a least-squares slope so sensor jitter can't fake it. Ordinary tilts are much slower than that.

3
It's a big drop.

At least 22° below where the lid was in the last 1.5 seconds.

4
Pause and it holds; lift the lid and it's gone.

Pause halfway and the fold waits right where it is. Lift the lid back up, or move the trackpad, click or press a key, and the blur clears from the top down in under half a second. Slow, careful closers are covered too: below 40° any steady downward motion counts.

Adjusting the screen: ignoredClosing the lid: folds

The power-off fade works the same way. A quick tap of the power button (lock or sleep) does nothing. It only plays when you hold the button. If the Mac stays on, the screen fades back.

Install in a minute

Mac Duo is free and open source. It isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm the first time you open it.

  1. Download & drag to ApplicationsOpen MacDuo.dmg and drag Mac Duo onto the Applications folder.
  2. Open it onceDouble-click Mac Duo. If macOS says it can't verify the developer, click Done, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Grant two optional permissionsScreen Recording lets the fold show your real desktop (one frame, kept in memory, never saved). Input Monitoring lets the app notice you holding the power button.
  4. Close your lidThat's it. The laptop icon in the menu bar has previews, sensitivity settings and Open at Login.
Prefer the Terminal? This removes the download quarantine flag in one step: 
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Mac Duo.app"

Questions

Which Macs does it work on?

The lid fold needs a MacBook with a lid angle sensor. That covers most Apple silicon MacBook Air and MacBook Pro models, plus the 16-inch MacBook Pro from 2019. Mac Duo checks for the sensor when it launches and tells you if your Mac doesn't have one. The glossy power-off fade works on any Mac running macOS 14 or later.

Will it fold when I just tilt my screen?

No. Readjustments are small or slow. A fold needs a fast (60°/s+), large drop that's already heading below 85°. If it ever starts by mistake, for example because you dipped the lid fast and then stopped, touch the trackpad and it unfolds. Choose Strict in the menu to be even pickier, or Relaxed to fold earlier.

Why does it want Screen Recording?

To blur your actual desktop in layers, Mac Duo grabs a single frame the moment your lid starts moving fast. The frame stays in memory, gets replaced the next time, and is never written to disk or sent anywhere. Without this permission Mac Duo raises the system's frosted-glass blur over the live screen instead, which can't deepen toward the hinge.

Does it change how my Mac sleeps or shuts down?

No. macOS still sleeps when the lid closes and still powers off when you hold the button. Mac Duo only draws on top during those moments. For a menu-bar Shut Down or Restart, it delays the app's own exit by about a second and a half so the fade can finish.

Does it drain my battery?

Hardly. While the lid sits still, it reads the hinge 15 times a second. That rises to 60 only while the lid is moving, and frames are drawn only during an animation.

Is this affiliated with Apple?

No. Mac Duo is an independent fan project inspired by the iPhone Duo's fold animation. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Apple.
